Understanding Hydration

Why water is the most underrated performance tool you have.

🧮 How Is My Water Target Calculated?

This calculator starts with the most widely used baseline: 33 ml of water per kg of body weight. A 70 kg person therefore needs about 2.3 liters as a foundation. From there, we add adjustments for the three biggest factors that increase fluid loss:

  • Exercise: +350 to +1,000 ml depending on duration and intensity — you lose 0.5–1 liter per hour through sweat during moderate exercise.
  • Climate: +250 to +500 ml in hot or humid weather, where sweat loss increases even at rest.
  • Special conditions: Pregnancy, breastfeeding, and fever all raise fluid needs significantly.

💧 Why Hydration Matters More Than You Think

Your body is roughly 60% water, and even mild dehydration (1–2% of body weight) has measurable effects:

  • Energy and focus: Mild dehydration reduces concentration, memory, and mood — often mistaken for tiredness or hunger.
  • Physical performance: Losing just 2% of body water can reduce exercise performance by 10–20%.
  • Weight management: Thirst is frequently confused with hunger. Drinking water before meals has been shown to reduce calorie intake.
  • Digestion and skin: Adequate water supports regular digestion, kidney function, and skin elasticity.

✅ Easy Habits to Hit Your Water Goal

  • Start your day with a glass: You wake up mildly dehydrated after 7–8 hours without fluids.
  • Keep a bottle visible: A 1-liter bottle on your desk is a constant reminder — finish it twice and you're done.
  • Link it to habits: Drink a glass with every meal, every bathroom break, and every coffee.
  • Use the urine test: Pale yellow = well hydrated. Dark yellow = drink more. Clear = possibly overdoing it.
  • Add flavor if needed: Lemon, cucumber, mint, or a splash of juice makes water more appealing without meaningful calories.

⚠️ Hydration Myths, Busted

  • "Coffee dehydrates you": False. Caffeine is a mild diuretic, but the water in coffee and tea more than compensates — they count toward your daily total.
  • "You must drink 8 glasses exactly": It's a rough average, not a rule. A 50 kg person and a 100 kg person have very different needs.
  • "More water is always better": Not true. Extreme overconsumption can dilute blood sodium (hyponatremia). Spread intake across the day — your kidneys process about 0.8–1 liter per hour.
  • "Thirst is a late warning": Partly true, but for healthy people, thirst plus pale-yellow urine is a reliable everyday guide.
FAQs

Hydration Questions, Answered

Quick answers to common water questions.

How much water should I drink a day?

A common guideline is 30–35 ml per kg of body weight. For a 70 kg person, that's about 2.1–2.5 liters daily. Exercise, hot weather, and pregnancy increase your needs.

Is 8 glasses of water a day enough?

The "8 glasses" rule (about 2 liters) is a rough average, but individual needs vary widely. Heavier people, athletes, and those in hot climates often need significantly more. Your weight-based target is more accurate.

Does coffee and tea count toward my water intake?

Yes. Despite mild caffeine, coffee and tea contribute to your daily hydration. Water-rich foods like fruits, vegetables, and soups also count — typically providing about 20% of daily fluid needs.

Can you drink too much water?

Yes, though it's rare. Drinking extreme amounts in a short time can cause hyponatremia (dangerously low sodium). Healthy kidneys handle about 0.8–1 liter per hour, so spread your intake across the day.

How much extra water should I drink when exercising?

Add roughly 500–750 ml for every 30–60 minutes of moderate exercise, and more for intense or sweaty sessions. Weighing yourself before and after shows exactly how much fluid you lost.

What are the signs of dehydration?

Early signs include thirst, dark yellow urine, dry mouth, fatigue, and headache. By the time you feel thirsty, you're already mildly dehydrated. Pale yellow urine is a good sign of proper hydration.
