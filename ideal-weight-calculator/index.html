Understanding Ideal Weight

Why there's no single "perfect" number.

📏 Why the BMI Range Matters More

Single-number formulas can't account for your frame size, muscle mass, or body composition. That's why health organizations recommend a range instead:

  • The healthy range = weights where your BMI falls between 18.5 and 24.9
  • It gives you flexibility — anywhere in the range is healthy
  • Muscular? You might healthily sit above the range (check body fat % instead)

⚠️ Important Limitations

  • These formulas were designed for adults — not children or teens.
  • They don't consider muscle mass, bone density, or body frame.
  • Athletes may weigh more than "ideal" while being very healthy.
  • Use these numbers as a general guide, not a strict target.
FAQs

Ideal Weight Questions, Answered

Quick answers to common questions.

What is my ideal body weight?

Ideal weight depends on your height and gender. Popular formulas like Devine, Robinson, Miller, and Hamwi give slightly different estimates, which is why a healthy range (based on BMI 18.5–24.9) is often more useful than a single number.

Which ideal weight formula is most accurate?

No single formula is perfect for everyone. The Devine formula is the most widely used in medicine, but all formulas are estimates. The healthy BMI range is generally the best guide for most people.

Why do different formulas give different results?

Each formula was developed using different populations and methods. That's why we show all four plus an average — your true ideal weight likely falls somewhere within that cluster.

Does ideal weight account for muscle mass?

No. These formulas are based on height and gender only. Muscular people may healthily weigh more than the 'ideal' number. Body fat percentage is a better measure for athletes.

Is my data saved when I use this calculator?

No. All calculations run entirely in your browser. Your height and results are never stored or sent anywhere.
