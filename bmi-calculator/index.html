🧮 How Is BMI Calculated?

BMI is calculated by dividing your weight in kilograms by your height in meters squared:

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²

For example, if you weigh 70 kg and are 1.75 m tall: 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 22.9 — which falls in the healthy range.

⚠️ Limitations of BMI

BMI is a helpful screening tool, but it has limits:

  • It doesn't distinguish between muscle and fat — athletes may show a high BMI with low body fat.
  • It doesn't account for age, gender, or body composition.
  • It may not be accurate for pregnant women, children, or the elderly.

For a complete picture of your health, combine BMI with other measures like body fat percentage — and always consult a healthcare professional.

Understanding BMI

What your Body Mass Index really tells you — and what it doesn't.

🧮 What Is BMI and How Is It Calculated?

BMI (Body Mass Index) is a simple screening tool that compares your weight to your height. It was developed in the 1830s by Belgian statistician Adolphe Quetelet and is still used worldwide by doctors and health organizations as a quick first check:

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²

For example, a person weighing 70 kg at 1.75 m tall has a BMI of 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 22.9 — right in the healthy range. The formula works the same for men and women, and it takes just seconds to calculate, which is exactly why it remains so popular nearly 200 years later.

⚠️ BMI Limitations You Should Know

BMI is useful, but it's not perfect. It doesn't measure body fat directly — it only compares weight and height. This means:

  • Athletes and muscular people often get labeled "overweight" or even "obese" despite having very low body fat. Muscle is denser than fat, so it weighs more.
  • Older adults may have a "healthy" BMI while carrying too much fat and too little muscle (called sarcopenic obesity).
  • Different ethnicities face health risks at different BMI levels. For example, Asian populations may face increased risk starting at BMI 23.
  • Children and teens need age- and sex-specific BMI charts — adult categories don't apply.

Bottom line: Use BMI as a starting point, not a diagnosis. For a fuller picture, pair it with body fat percentage, waist measurement, and how you actually feel.

📏 BMI vs Body Fat Percentage: Which Is Better?

These two measurements answer different questions. BMI tells you if your weight is appropriate for your height. Body fat percentage tells you what your weight is actually made of — fat versus muscle, bone, and water.

Two people can have the exact same BMI of 27: one might be a muscular athlete with 12% body fat, while the other is sedentary with 32% body fat. Same BMI, completely different health profiles. If you exercise regularly, body fat percentage is usually the more meaningful number to track.

✅ How to Improve Your BMI Safely

  • If you're above the healthy range: A modest calorie deficit of 400–500 kcal per day leads to steady, sustainable fat loss of about 0.5 kg per week. Combine this with protein-rich meals and regular walking or exercise.
  • If you're below the healthy range: Add 250–300 calories per day with nutrient-dense foods like nuts, eggs, dairy, and whole grains. Strength training helps you gain muscle rather than just fat.
  • Either way: Aim for gradual change — 0.25 to 0.5 kg per week. Rapid changes are harder to sustain and can harm your health.
  • Recheck your BMI every 4–6 weeks to track progress, and remember that how you feel matters as much as the number.
FAQs

BMI Questions, Answered

Quick answers to the most common BMI questions.

What is BMI?

BMI (Body Mass Index) is a simple measure that uses your height and weight to estimate whether you are underweight, at a healthy weight, overweight, or obese.

What is a healthy BMI range?

According to the WHO, a healthy BMI for adults is between 18.5 and 24.9. Below 18.5 is underweight, 25–29.9 is overweight, and 30 or above is obese.

Is BMI accurate for everyone?

BMI is a useful screening tool, but it does not measure body fat directly. Athletes and muscular people may have a high BMI without excess fat. Always consult a healthcare professional for a full assessment.
