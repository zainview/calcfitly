Understanding Heart Rate Zones

Train at the right intensity — and stop guessing how hard to push.

🧮 How Are Heart Rate Zones Calculated?

Your zones are based on your maximum heart rate (MHR) — the fastest your heart can beat during all-out effort. The classic estimate is simple:

  • Max HR = 220 − your age
  • A 30-year-old: 220 − 30 = 190 bpm

Each zone is then a percentage band of that max. If you entered your resting heart rate, this calculator uses the more personalized Karvonen method, which factors in your heart rate reserve (max HR − resting HR) — fitter people have lower resting rates and therefore different zone boundaries.

🔥 The Truth About the "Fat Burning Zone"

Zone 2 is famous as the "fat burning zone" — and it's true that a higher percentage of calories come from fat at this intensity. But here's the catch:

  • At Zone 2, roughly 50–60% of your calories come from fat
  • At Zone 4, only ~25% comes from fat — but you're burning nearly twice as many total calories per minute
  • Result: you often burn more total fat per hour in higher zones anyway

Bottom line: For fat loss, your daily calorie deficit matters far more than which zone you train in. Zone 2's real superpower is that it's sustainable — you can do it daily without exhaustion.

📅 How to Structure Your Training Week

Elite endurance athletes follow the 80/20 rule — and it works for beginners too:

  • ~80% of your time in Zones 1–2: Easy walking, jogging, or cycling. This builds your aerobic engine and recovery capacity.
  • ~20% in Zones 3–5: Tempo runs, intervals, hill sprints. This drives fitness gains.

The most common mistake? Living in Zone 3 — the "gray zone" where every workout feels moderately hard but never truly easy or truly challenging. Polarizing your intensity gets better results with less fatigue.

⌚ Tips for Accurate Heart Rate Training

  • Use a chest strap for intervals: Wrist sensors lag 10–20 seconds during rapid heart rate changes — fine for steady runs, unreliable for sprints.
  • Measure resting HR correctly: First thing in the morning, before getting out of bed, for 3–5 days and take the average.
  • Expect drift in heat: Heart rate runs 5–10 bpm higher in hot, humid weather at the same pace — adjust zones or slow down.
  • Watch the trend, not the day: Poor sleep, stress, caffeine, and illness all raise heart rate. One odd reading means nothing; a rising weekly trend means rest.
  • Re-test every few months: As fitness improves, your zones shift — recalculate to keep training accurate.
FAQs

Heart Rate Questions, Answered

Quick answers to common questions.

What is my maximum heart rate?

The most common estimate is 220 minus your age. A 30-year-old has an estimated max heart rate of 190 bpm. This is a population average — individual max heart rates can vary by 10–15 bpm.

What heart rate zone burns the most fat?

Zone 2 (60–70% of max heart rate) burns the highest percentage of calories from fat. However, higher zones burn more total calories per minute, so both approaches work for fat loss — total calorie deficit matters most.

What is a normal resting heart rate?

For most adults, 60–100 bpm is normal. Fit individuals often rest at 50–60 bpm, and elite endurance athletes can be in the 40s. A lower resting heart rate generally indicates better cardiovascular fitness.

How long should I train in each zone?

A common guideline is the 80/20 rule: about 80% of your training time in Zones 1–2 (easy) and 20% in Zones 3–5 (moderate to hard). Most beginners do the opposite — too much moderate effort, not enough truly easy work.

Is the 220-minus-age formula accurate?

It's a reasonable estimate for most people, but it can be off by 10–15 bpm in either direction. If you train seriously, a field test (like a hard 5K effort) or lab test gives a more personal number.

What is a dangerous heart rate during exercise?

Consistently exceeding your estimated max heart rate, or experiencing chest pain, dizziness, or unusual shortness of breath during exercise, warrants stopping and consulting a doctor. Healthy people training in Zones 1–4 are generally safe.
