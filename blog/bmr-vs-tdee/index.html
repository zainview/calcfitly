BMR vs TDEE: What's the Difference? (Simple Guide)

They sound similar, they're closely related — and confusing them can quietly sabotage your entire diet. Here's the clear difference between the two most important numbers in fitness.

📅 Oct 8, 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read ✍️ CalcFitly Team
In this guide: What is BMR? (quick recap) What is TDEE? (quick recap) How the two numbers connect BMR vs TDEE: side-by-side table Which number should you use? A worked example 5 common mistakes FAQs

Start any fitness journey and two numbers will show up almost immediately: BMR and TDEE. They sound alike. They're mathematically connected. And they are constantly mixed up — sometimes with painful results.

Eating at your BMR when you meant to eat at your TDEE, for example, means accidentally crash-dieting: exhausted, hungry, and losing muscle instead of fat. Let's make sure that never happens to you.

What Is BMR? (The Quick Version)

BMR stands for Basal Metabolic Rate — the number of calories your body burns at complete rest, just to keep you alive. Think of it like a car idling at a red light: the engine is on, fuel is burning, but the car isn't going anywhere.

Your BMR covers the essentials:

  • Heart beating and blood circulating
  • Lungs breathing
  • Brain and organs functioning
  • Body temperature regulation
  • Cell repair and basic maintenance

For most adults, BMR falls somewhere between 1,200 and 2,000 calories per day. It's calculated from your age, sex, height, and weight — we covered the full math in our guide: What Is BMR? Basal Metabolic Rate Explained. You can also get yours instantly with the free BMR calculator.

What Is TDEE? (The Quick Version)

TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure — the total calories your body burns in a full, real day. It takes your BMR and adds everything else:

  • Walking, standing, and general daily movement
  • Your workouts and sports
  • Digesting and processing food
  • Fidgeting, chores, and all the small stuff

Because it includes real life, your TDEE is always higher than your BMR — typically by 20% to 90%, depending on how active you are. Full breakdown here: What Is TDEE? Total Daily Energy Expenditure Explained.

How the Two Numbers Connect

Here's the key insight most people miss: TDEE is built from BMR. They aren't two unrelated numbers — one is the foundation of the other.

The Master Formula
TDEE = BMR × Activity Factor
Your resting burn, multiplied by how much you actually move.

The activity factor works like this:

Activity Level Multiplier Typical Lifestyle
Sedentary× 1.2Desk job, little or no exercise
Light× 1.375Exercise 1–3 days per week
Moderate× 1.55Exercise 3–5 days per week
Active× 1.725Hard exercise 6–7 days per week
Very Active× 1.9Physical job plus daily training

Which Number Should You Actually Use?

Simple rule: plan with TDEE, respect BMR as your floor.

  • Setting your daily calories? Always start from TDEE. It's the only number that reflects your actual life.
  • Losing weight? Eat 400–500 kcal below your TDEE — this gives roughly 0.5 kg of fat loss per week, safely. More detail in our guide on how to lose weight safely.
  • Gaining muscle? Eat 250–300 kcal above your TDEE.
  • Maintaining? Eat at your TDEE.

And where does BMR fit in? As a safety boundary. Consistently eating below your BMR means your body isn't getting enough energy for its most basic functions — that's when muscle loss, fatigue, and metabolic slowdown creep in.

A Worked Example: Same Person, Both Numbers

Let's take Ahmed — 25 years old, 175 cm, 70 kg, who trains moderately 3–5 days a week:

📐 Step-by-Step Calculation
Step 1 — BMR (Mifflin-St Jeor): (10 × 70) + (6.25 × 175) − (5 × 25) + 5 = 1,674 kcal
Step 2 — Activity multiplier: Moderate = × 1.55
Step 3 — TDEE: 1,674 × 1.55 = 2,595 kcal
Step 4 — Weight loss target: 2,595 − 500 = 2,095 kcal
✅ Ahmed's BMR: 1,674 · TDEE: 2,595 · Diet target: ~2,095 kcal/day

Notice what happens if Ahmed had confused the two numbers: eating at his BMR of 1,674 instead of his proper target of 2,095 would mean an accidental 920-calorie deficit — nearly double what's safe. That's the mix-up this article exists to prevent.

5 Common BMR and TDEE Mistakes

  1. Dieting at your BMR instead of below your TDEE. The most common and most damaging error — it creates an extreme deficit without you realizing it.
  2. Overestimating your activity level. Three gym sessions a week is "Moderate," not "Active." When in doubt, pick one level lower and adjust based on results.
  3. Eating below BMR for months. Occasional low days are fine; chronic under-eating costs you muscle, energy, and hormones.
  4. Never recalculating. Lost 5 kg? Your BMR and TDEE both dropped. Recalculate every 4–6 weeks during a diet.
  5. Treating TDEE as exact truth. It's a well-researched estimate, not gospel. Track your real-world results for 2–3 weeks and adjust by 100–200 kcal if needed.

The Bottom Line

BMR is your survival number. TDEE is your real-life number. BMR tells you what your body needs just to exist; TDEE tells you what it actually uses living your life. Plan your diet around TDEE, and treat BMR as the floor you don't cross for long.
