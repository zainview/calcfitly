What Is TDEE? Total Daily Energy Expenditure Explained

TDEE is the total number of calories you burn every single day — and the most important number for reaching any weight goal. Here's how to find yours.

📅 Sep 26, 2026 ⏱️ 7 min read ✍️ CalcFitly Team
In this guide: What is TDEE? The 4 parts that make up your TDEE How to calculate your TDEE Using TDEE for weight loss or muscle gain FAQs

In our last guide, we explained what BMR is — the calories you burn at complete rest. But here's the thing: you don't lie in bed all day. You walk, work, cook, exercise, and fidget. All of that burns calories too.

Add everything together and you get TDEE — the single most useful number in fitness.

What Is TDEE?

TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is the total number of calories your body burns in 24 hours — including rest, movement, exercise, and even digestion.

Think of it this way:

  • 🔋 BMR = your phone's battery drain on standby mode
  • 📱 TDEE = your battery drain on a full day of actual use

Why does TDEE matter so much? Because it's your maintenance number:

  • Eat at your TDEE → weight stays the same
  • Eat below your TDEE → you lose weight
  • Eat above your TDEE → you gain weight

Every diet plan — weight loss, muscle gain, or maintenance — starts with this one number.

The 4 Parts That Make Up Your TDEE

Your daily calorie burn comes from four sources:

Component What It Is Share of TDEE
BMR Rest calories — heartbeat, breathing, brain 60–75%
NEAT Daily movement — walking, chores, fidgeting 15–30%
TEF Digesting and processing food ~10%
EAT Planned exercise — gym, running, sports 5–10%

Notice something surprising? Exercise (EAT) is the smallest piece for most people. Your daily movement (NEAT) — taking stairs, walking to the kitchen, doing housework — often burns more calories than your workout. This is why 10,000 steps a day can rival a gym session for weight loss.

How to Calculate Your TDEE

It's a simple two-step process:

The TDEE Formula
TDEE = BMR × Activity Factor
First find your BMR, then multiply by your activity level below.

Step 1: Find Your BMR

Use the Mifflin-St Jeor formula we covered in our BMR guide, or get it instantly with our BMR calculator.

Step 2: Multiply by Your Activity Level

Activity Level Lifestyle Multiply BMR By
Sedentary Desk job, little or no exercise × 1.2
Lightly Active Light exercise 1–3 days/week × 1.375
Moderately Active Exercise 3–5 days/week × 1.55
Very Active Hard exercise 6–7 days/week × 1.725
Extra Active Physical job + daily training × 1.9

Honest tip: Most people overestimate their activity. If you're unsure between two levels, pick the lower one — it's better to start conservative and adjust up.

🧮 Example: Same 30-Year-Old Man From Our BMR Guide
His BMR: 1,699 calories (calculated in the BMR article)
His lifestyle: Works out 4 days a week → Moderately Active
Calculation: 1,699 × 1.55
✅ TDEE = 2,633 calories per day (his maintenance level)

Using TDEE for Weight Loss or Muscle Gain

Once you know your TDEE, reaching your goal is simple arithmetic:

🎯 For Weight Loss

Eat 300–500 calories below your TDEE. In our example: 2,633 − 500 = ~2,100 calories/day for steady fat loss of about 0.5 kg per week. Remember the golden rule from our BMR guide — never eat below your BMR for long periods. For a complete breakdown, read how many calories you should eat a day.

💪 For Muscle Gain

Eat 200–300 calories above your TDEE and keep protein high — our protein calculator can set your daily target. A small surplus builds muscle; a huge surplus mostly builds fat.

⚖️ For Maintenance

Eat at your TDEE. Simple. This is also a smart strategy between dieting phases to let your metabolism recover.

One important habit: Recalculate your TDEE every 4–6 weeks. As your weight changes, your TDEE changes too — the calories that caused weight loss at 80 kg may be maintenance calories at 74 kg.

The Bottom Line

BMR tells you what you burn at rest. TDEE tells you what you burn living your actual life — and it's the number every weight goal is built on. Find yours, eat slightly below it to lose fat or slightly above it to build muscle, and recheck it as your body changes. 💚

⚠️ A quick note: TDEE is an estimate — treat it as a starting point, not gospel. Track your weight for 2–3 weeks and adjust by 100–200 calories if results don't match expectations. For medical conditions, consult a healthcare professional.
