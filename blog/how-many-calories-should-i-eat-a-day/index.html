How Many Calories Should I Eat a Day?

📅 September 19, 2026 · ⏱️ 7 min read · ✍️ CalcFitly Team

It's the most Googled nutrition question in the world — and for good reason. Whether you want to lose weight, gain muscle, or simply stay healthy, everything starts with one number: how many calories your body actually needs each day.

The honest answer? It depends on you — your age, gender, size, and how much you move. But don't worry. By the end of this guide, you'll know exactly how to find your personal number (and how to adjust it for your goal).

⚡ First, What Is a Calorie?

A calorie is simply a unit of energy. Your body burns calories for everything — breathing, thinking, walking, digesting food, even sleeping. The food you eat is the fuel; your daily life is the engine burning it.

  • Eat more calories than you burn → your body stores the extra (weight gain)
  • Eat fewer calories than you burn → your body uses stored energy (weight loss)
  • Eat about the same → your weight stays stable (maintenance)

This is called energy balance, and it's the foundation of every diet that has ever worked.

Important: these are population averages. A 110 kg man and a 60 kg man of the same age need very different amounts of food. That's why calculating your personal number matters — more on that below.

🧬 What Determines Your Calorie Needs?

Five main factors decide how much energy your body burns daily:

  • Body size: Bigger bodies burn more — more cells to power. Weight is the biggest single factor.
  • Muscle mass: Muscle burns more calories than fat, even at rest. Two people at the same weight can have different needs.
  • Age: Needs typically drop 100–200 calories per decade after 30, mainly due to muscle loss.
  • Gender: Men generally need more calories due to larger bodies and higher muscle mass.
  • Activity level: The most controllable factor. A desk worker and a construction worker of the same size can differ by 600+ calories a day.

🧮 How to Calculate YOUR Number (2 Steps)

Finding your personal calorie target takes two steps:

Step 1 — Find your BMR (calories burned at rest)
BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age + 5 (men) or − 161 (women)
This is the Mifflin-St Jeor equation — the most accurate formula for most people
Step 2 — Multiply by your activity level
TDEE = BMR × 1.2 (sedentary) up to × 1.9 (very active)
Example: BMR 1,600 × 1.375 (light exercise) = 2,200 calories/day to maintain

The result is your TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) — the number of calories you need to maintain your current weight. Our free calorie calculator does both steps instantly, and you can dive deeper with our TDEE calculator and BMR calculator.

📉 Calories for Weight Loss

To lose weight, eat below your TDEE. The sweet spot for most people:

  • Deficit of 400–500 calories/day → lose roughly 0.5 kg (1 lb) per week
  • This pace is slow enough to protect muscle, keep energy stable, and actually stick to

⚠️ Avoid going below 1,200 calories (women) or 1,500 calories (men) without medical supervision. Crash dieting causes muscle loss, fatigue, and rebound weight gain.

📈 Calories for Muscle Gain

Building muscle requires eating above your TDEE — but not by much:

  • Surplus of 200–300 calories/day is enough for steady muscle growth
  • Bigger surpluses mostly add fat, not extra muscle
  • Pair your surplus with adequate protein (1.6–2.2 g per kg) — our protein calculator gives you your exact target

💡 Remember: your calorie needs change as your weight changes. Recalculate every 4–6 weeks, or after every 2–3 kg lost or gained.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories should I eat a day to lose weight?

Eat about 400–500 calories below your TDEE (maintenance calories). For most women that's roughly 1,400–1,700 per day; for most men, 1,800–2,200. This pace loses about 0.5 kg per week while protecting muscle and energy.

Is 1,200 calories a day enough?

For most adults, no. 1,200 calories is below the basal metabolic rate of many people and can cause muscle loss, fatigue, nutrient deficiencies, and metabolic slowdown. Only short, medically supervised diets should go this low.

How many calories should a woman eat per day?

General guidelines suggest 1,600–2,000 calories daily for sedentary to moderately active women, and up to 2,400 for very active women. Your exact number depends on your age, weight, height, and activity level.

How many calories should a man eat per day?

General guidelines suggest 2,000–2,500 calories daily for sedentary to moderately active men, and up to 3,000 for very active men. Individual needs vary with body size, age, and activity.

Do calorie needs decrease with age?

Yes, mostly because muscle mass declines with age and people tend to move less. Needs typically drop by roughly 100–200 calories per decade after age 30. Strength training helps prevent this decline.
