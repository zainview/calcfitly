BMI Formula: How to Calculate BMI (With Examples)

The exact math behind Body Mass Index — in kilograms and in pounds — with worked examples you can follow in under a minute.

The BMI Formula, Explained Simply

BMI stands for Body Mass Index. It's a single number that compares your weight to your height, and it's been used since the 1830s as a quick screening tool for weight categories.

The core idea is simple:

The Core BMI Formula
BMI = weight ÷ height²
Your weight divided by your height multiplied by itself. That's it.

The only tricky part? The formula changes slightly depending on whether you measure in kilograms and meters, or pounds and inches. Let's cover both. (New to BMI entirely? Start with our beginner guide: What Is BMI?)

BMI Formula in kg and Meters (Metric)

This is the original, standard version of the formula used worldwide:

Metric BMI Formula
BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²
Weight in kilograms, divided by height in meters squared.

Example: Calculating BMI in kg

Let's say Priya weighs 70 kg and is 1.75 m tall. Here's the calculation step by step:

📐 Worked Example (Metric)
Step 1: Square the height → 1.75 × 1.75 = 3.0625
Step 2: Divide weight by that number → 70 ÷ 3.0625 = 22.86
Step 3: Round to one decimal → 22.9
✅ Priya's BMI = 22.9 (healthy range)

Quick tip: If your height is in centimeters, convert it to meters first by dividing by 100. So 175 cm = 1.75 m. This is the most common mistake people make — more on that below.

BMI Formula in Pounds and Inches (Imperial)

If you measure in pounds and inches, the formula needs one extra ingredient — the number 703:

Imperial BMI Formula
BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)²
Multiply your weight in pounds by 703, then divide by your height in inches squared.

Example: Calculating BMI in pounds

Let's say Rahul weighs 154 lb and is 5'8" tall. First, convert his height to inches: 5 feet × 12 + 8 = 68 inches. Now the calculation:

📐 Worked Example (Imperial)
Step 1: Convert height to inches → 5'8" = 68 in
Step 2: Multiply weight by 703 → 154 × 703 = 108,262
Step 3: Square the height → 68 × 68 = 4,624
Step 4: Divide → 108,262 ÷ 4,624 = 23.4
✅ Rahul's BMI = 23.4 (healthy range)

Notice something? 154 lb is 70 kg, and 5'8" is 1.73 m — Rahul and Priya are nearly the same size, and both formulas give nearly the same answer. The math checks out. ✅

BMI Chart: What Your Number Means

Once you've calculated your BMI, here's how to read it. These are the standard WHO categories for adults aged 20 and over:

BMI Range Category What It Means
Below 18.5 Underweight May indicate insufficient nutrition or an underlying condition
18.5 – 24.9 Healthy Weight Associated with the lowest health risk for most adults
25.0 – 29.9 Overweight Slightly elevated risk; worth reviewing diet and activity
30.0 – 34.9 Obese (Class 1) Increased risk of heart disease, diabetes, and joint issues
35.0 – 39.9 Obese (Class 2) High risk; professional guidance recommended
40.0 and above Obese (Class 3) Very high risk; medical support strongly advised

Why Does the Imperial Formula Use 703?

Fair question — it seems like a random number. It's not.

The number 703 is a unit conversion factor. One kilogram is about 2.2046 pounds, and one meter is about 39.37 inches. When you do the math to convert the metric formula into pounds and inches, the conversion works out to roughly 703 (703.07, to be precise).

Without 703, the imperial formula would give you a number about 703 times too small. A BMI of 23 would show up as 0.033 — not very useful! So the 703 simply makes pounds and inches speak the same language as kilograms and meters.

5 Common BMI Calculation Mistakes

The formula is simple, but these slip-ups trip people up all the time:

  1. Using centimeters instead of meters. 175 cm must become 1.75 m before you square it. Squaring 175 gives a wildly wrong answer.
  2. Forgetting to square the height. "Height²" means height × height — not height × 2. 1.75² = 3.06, not 3.5.
  3. Mixing units. Kilograms with inches, or pounds with meters, won't work. Pick one system and stick with it.
  4. Using feet instead of inches. In the imperial formula, 5'8" must become 68 inches — not 5.8 or 5.67.
  5. Rounding too early. Keep the decimals until the final step, then round to one decimal place.

What BMI Doesn't Tell You

The BMI formula only uses two inputs — weight and height. That makes it fast, but also limited. It can't tell the difference between muscle and fat. A muscular athlete and a sedentary person with the same height and weight get the same BMI, even though their body compositions are completely different.

That's why BMI works best as a starting point, not a final verdict. For a fuller picture, pair it with your body fat percentage, and use our TDEE calculator to understand your daily energy needs. We cover this in more detail in What Is BMI? Complete Guide for Beginners.

⚠️ A quick note: BMI is a screening tool, not a diagnosis. If your result concerns you, talk to a qualified healthcare professional who can assess your full health picture.
