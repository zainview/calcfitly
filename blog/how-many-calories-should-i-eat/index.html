How Many Calories Should I Eat a Day? (By Age, Gender & Goal)

The quick answer, the honest math, and your personal number — all in one simple guide.

📅 Sep 30, 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read ✍️ CalcFitly Team
In this guide: The quick answer What decides your calorie needs? Calories by goal: lose, maintain, or gain How to find your personal number Safe minimums you shouldn't cross FAQs

It's the most-asked nutrition question on the internet — and most answers are either too vague ("it depends!") or too complicated. Let's fix that. In the next few minutes, you'll get the general answer and learn how to find your exact personal number. 💚

The Quick Answer

For weight maintenance, health authorities like the FDA and NHS use these daily averages:

Group Sedentary Moderately Active Active
Women (19–30) 1,800–2,000 2,000–2,200 2,400
Women (31–50) 1,800 2,000 2,200
Women (51+) 1,600 1,800 2,000–2,200
Men (19–30) 2,400 2,600–2,800 3,000
Men (31–50) 2,200 2,400–2,600 2,800–3,000
Men (51+) 2,000 2,200–2,400 2,400–2,800

That's why food labels say "based on a 2,000 calorie diet" — it's a rough middle ground. But your number depends on much more than an average. 👇

What Decides Your Calorie Needs?

Four things control how many calories you burn each day:

  • ⚖️ Body size: Bigger bodies burn more — a 90 kg person needs more fuel than a 60 kg person
  • 🎂 Age: Needs drop ~1–2% per decade after 30, mostly due to muscle loss
  • 🚻 Gender: Men typically burn 200–400 more calories daily due to higher muscle mass
  • 🏃 Activity: The biggest lever — an active person can burn 600+ more calories than a sedentary one of the same size

Your starting point is your BMR — the calories you burn at complete rest (we covered it in What Is BMR?). Multiply that by your activity level and you get your TDEE — your true daily burn (full guide: What Is TDEE?).

Calories by Goal: Lose, Maintain, or Gain

Once you know your TDEE, your goal decides the adjustment:

Goal Daily Calories Expected Result
📉 Lose weight TDEE − 500 ~0.5 kg fat loss per week
⚖️ Maintain = TDEE Weight stays stable
📈 Gain muscle TDEE + 300 Slow, lean muscle gain
🧮 A Real Example
Person: 35-year-old woman, 70 kg, moderately active
Her TDEE: ~2,100 calories
To lose weight: 2,100 − 500 = 1,600 calories/day
To gain muscle: 2,100 + 300 = 2,400 calories/day
✅ Same person, three different targets — the goal decides the number.

How to Find Your Personal Number

Three simple steps:

  1. Calculate your TDEE using our calorie calculator (or the TDEE calculator directly)
  2. Adjust for your goal — minus 500 to lose, plus 300 to gain, or stay level to maintain
  3. Test for 2–3 weeks — if your weight isn't moving as expected, adjust by 100–200 calories and re-test

One honest warning: fitness trackers and machines often overestimate exercise burn by 20–50%. If your target already includes activity, don't "eat back" workout calories — that double-counting stalls more diets than any food does.

Safe Minimums You Shouldn't Cross

More deficit ≠ faster results. Below these lines, your body starts burning muscle and slowing metabolism:

Never Go Below (Without Medical Supervision)
Women: 1,200 kcal · Men: 1,500 kcal
Below this, fatigue, hair loss, hormone issues, and rebound weight gain become likely.

And a gentle reminder: calories are only half the story. What you eat matters too — 1,600 calories of protein-rich whole foods will keep you far fuller than 1,600 calories of snacks. Check your ideal split with our macro calculator.

The Bottom Line

Most women need 1,600–2,400 calories daily; most men need 2,000–3,000 — depending on size, age, and activity. Your personal number = your TDEE adjusted for your goal. Calculate it once, eat to it consistently, and let the math do the work. 💚

⚠️ A quick note: These figures are for generally healthy adults. Pregnancy, breastfeeding, medical conditions, and a history of eating disorders all change calorie needs — consult a healthcare professional for personal advice.
