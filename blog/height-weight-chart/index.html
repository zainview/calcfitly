Height Weight Chart: Ideal Weight by Height (Men & Women)

📅 September 21, 2026 · ⏱️ 6 min read · ✍️ CalcFitly Team

Wondering "what should my weight be for my height?" You're not alone — it's one of the most asked health questions in the world. The quick answer: a healthy weight for your height is the range where your BMI falls between 18.5 and 24.9. For example, if you're 5'6" (168 cm), your healthy weight range is roughly 52–70 kg.

Below you'll find the complete height weight chart for adults — in both kilograms and pounds — plus how to use it, where it falls short, and how to find your personal ideal weight.

👨‍👩 Is the Chart Different for Men and Women?

The healthy range itself is the same for men and women — it's based purely on height. But where your ideal weight sits within that range often differs:

  • Men naturally carry more muscle mass and bone density, so their ideal weight typically sits toward the higher end of the range.
  • Women naturally carry a higher percentage of essential body fat, so their ideal weight often sits toward the middle of the range.
  • Body frame matters too: someone with a large frame (broader shoulders, thicker wrists) can healthily weigh 5–8% more than someone with a small frame at the same height.

🧮 How Is the Height Weight Chart Calculated?

The chart is built on the BMI formula: weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². Health authorities define a healthy BMI as 18.5–24.9, so the chart simply works backwards:

  • Lower end = height² × 18.5 (the lightest healthy weight)
  • Upper end = height² × 24.9 (the heaviest healthy weight)

⚠️ Where the Height Weight Chart Falls Short

The chart is a great starting point — but it's not the final word. Here's what it doesn't see:

  • Muscle vs fat: Muscle is denser than fat, so fit, muscular people can exceed the "healthy" range while being in excellent shape.
  • Body composition: Two people at the same height and weight can look completely different. That's why your body fat percentage tells a deeper story than weight alone.
  • Age: We naturally lose muscle and gain fat as we age, even at the same weight.
  • Children and teens: Kids are still growing, so adult charts don't apply to them. A child's healthy weight is measured with age-specific growth charts — always check with a pediatrician.

🎯 Found Your Range? Here's What to Do Next

If you're inside the healthy range: Great! Focus on maintaining — eat at your daily calorie needs, stay active, and keep an eye on body composition.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What should my weight be for my height?

Your healthy weight depends on your height. For example, someone 5'4" (163 cm) should weigh roughly 49–66 kg, and someone 5'10" (178 cm) should weigh roughly 59–79 kg. These ranges are based on the healthy BMI range of 18.5–24.9.

What is the ideal weight for 5'6" (168 cm)?

For a height of 5'6" (168 cm), the healthy weight range is approximately 52–70 kg (115–154 lbs). The midpoint, around 60–62 kg, is often considered the ideal weight for a medium body frame.

Is the height weight chart different for men and women?

The healthy weight range based on BMI is the same for both, but men typically carry more muscle mass, so their ideal weight often sits at the higher end of the range, while women's ideal weight often sits toward the middle or lower end.

How accurate is a height weight chart?

A height weight chart is a useful starting point, but it doesn't account for muscle mass, body frame size, age, or fat distribution. Athletes and muscular people may healthily weigh more than the chart suggests. Pair it with body fat percentage for a complete picture.

Does the height weight chart apply to children?

No. Children and teens are still growing, so their healthy weight is assessed using age-and-gender-specific growth charts (BMI-for-age percentiles), not adult charts. Always consult a pediatrician about a child's weight.

What if my weight is outside the healthy range?

Don't panic. Use it as a starting point: check your BMI and body fat percentage, look at your daily calorie intake, and aim for gradual change — 0.5 kg per week is a safe, sustainable pace. Consult a healthcare professional for personalized advice.
