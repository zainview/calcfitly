How Much Water Should You Drink a Day? (By Weight & Activity)

Is the 8-glass rule real? Here's what science actually says about daily water needs — plus how to calculate yours.

📅 Oct 1, 2026 ⏱️ 7 min read ✍️ CalcFitly Team
In this guide: The quick answer Is the 8-glass rule a myth? Water needs by body weight When you need more water Signs you're not drinking enough FAQs

You've heard it a thousand times: "Drink 8 glasses of water a day!" But here's a surprising fact — that rule has almost no scientific basis, and your real need could be much higher (or a little lower).

Let's find out what the research actually says, and more importantly — how to calculate your number. 💧

The Quick Answer

The U.S. National Academies of Sciences suggests these total daily water intakes (from all drinks and food combined):

Group Total Daily Water From Drinks Alone
👩 Women ~2.7 liters ~2.0–2.2 liters
👨 Men ~3.7 liters ~2.5–3.0 liters

Remember: about 20% of your water comes from food — fruits, vegetables, soups, and even rice and bread contribute. So you don't need to drink all 3.7 liters!

Is the 8-Glass Rule a Myth?

Mostly, yes. The "8×8 rule" (eight 8-ounce glasses ≈ 2 liters) has no strong research behind it. It likely came from a misreading of a 1945 report — which actually said most water comes from food, a detail everyone forgot.

The truth is simpler and smarter: your body size and lifestyle decide your need. A 55 kg woman at a desk job and a 95 kg man training in summer heat have completely different requirements.

Water Needs by Body Weight

A widely used formula estimates daily needs from your weight:

The Body Weight Formula
Water (ml) = your weight (kg) × 30–35
Use 30 for sedentary days, 35 for active days or hot weather.
🧮 Quick Examples
60 kg person: 60 × 30–35 = 1.8 – 2.1 liters
75 kg person: 75 × 30–35 = 2.2 – 2.6 liters
90 kg person: 90 × 30–35 = 2.7 – 3.2 liters
✅ Heavier body = more water needed. Simple as that.

When You Need More Water

Your baseline goes up in these situations:

  • 🏋️ Exercise: Add 500–1,000 ml per hour of sweaty activity — and sip during, not just after
  • 🌡️ Hot or humid weather: Add 500 ml–1 liter on very hot days, even without exercise
  • 🤒 Fever, vomiting, or diarrhea: Your body loses fluid fast — replace it steadily
  • 🤰 Pregnancy & breastfeeding: Needs rise by roughly 300–700 ml daily
  • 🏔️ High altitude: Faster breathing means more water loss through your breath

And yes — tea, coffee, and milk count toward your intake. The old "coffee dehydrates you" myth has been debunked; the mild caffeine effect doesn't cancel the fluid.

Signs You're Not Drinking Enough

Your body has a built-in hydration gauge — you just need to read it:

Sign What It Means
🚽 Pale yellow urine ✅ Well hydrated — keep it up
🌕 Dark yellow / amber urine ⚠️ Drink more water soon
👄 Dry mouth & constant thirst ⚠️ Already mildly dehydrated
😴 Fatigue, headache, poor focus ⚠️ Common dehydration symptoms
🚽 Clear urine + bathroom every hour 💧 Possibly overdoing it — ease off

Bonus for weight loss: drinking ~500 ml of water 30 minutes before meals has been shown to reduce how much you eat. Pair good hydration with the right calorie target and safe weight loss plan for the best results.

The Bottom Line

Forget the rigid 8-glass rule. Most women need around 2–2.7 liters and most men 2.5–3.7 liters of total water daily — adjusted up for exercise, heat, and body size. Use the 30–35 ml per kg formula, watch your urine color, and let your body guide the rest. 💚

⚠️ A quick note: People with kidney disease, heart failure, or those on fluid restrictions should follow their doctor's guidance on water intake — more is not always better in these conditions.
