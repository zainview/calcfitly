Understanding Calories

The energy math behind every body goal.

🧮 How Are My Calories Calculated?

This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation — the formula the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics considers the most accurate for estimating your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR):

  • Men: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) + 5
  • Women: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) − 161

Your BMR is then multiplied by an activity factor (1.2 to 1.9) to get your TDEE — your true daily calorie burn. That maintenance number is the foundation for every goal: eat below it to lose, at it to maintain, or above it to gain.

✅ Tips for Hitting Your Calorie Target

  • Track for 2–4 weeks: Use a food tracking app to learn what your target actually looks like on a plate. Most people are surprised at first.
  • Prioritize protein: It's the most filling macronutrient and protects muscle during weight loss. Aim for a palm-sized portion at every meal.
  • Don't drink your calories: Sodas, juices, and sweet coffees can add 300–500 kcal without making you feel full.
  • Plan for real life: Birthdays and dinners out happen. One higher-calorie day won't ruin your week — consistency over time is what matters.
  • Recalculate as you change: Every 5 kg of weight change shifts your needs. Recheck every 4–6 weeks.

❌ Common Calorie Mistakes

  • Overestimating activity: An hour at the gym doesn't cancel out 8 hours of sitting. When in doubt, pick one activity level lower.
  • Forgetting weekend eating: Five disciplined weekdays can be undone by two unstructured weekend days.
  • Trusting machine calorie counts: Gym equipment and fitness watches often overestimate burn by 20–50%.
  • Going too low: Eating far below your needs slows progress long-term by costing you muscle and energy. Never go below 1,200 kcal (women) or 1,500 kcal (men) without medical guidance.
FAQs

Calorie Questions, Answered

Quick answers to common calorie questions.

How many calories should I eat per day?

It depends on your age, gender, weight, height, and activity level. Most adult women need 1,600–2,400 kcal daily, and most adult men need 2,000–3,000 kcal. Use this calculator for your personal number.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

Eat about 500 kcal below your maintenance calories (TDEE). This creates a deficit that leads to roughly 0.5 kg of fat loss per week. Never go below 1,200 kcal (women) or 1,500 kcal (men) without medical supervision.

What is the Mifflin-St Jeor equation?

It's the formula used to estimate your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) — the calories you burn at rest. The Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics considers it the most accurate BMR formula for most people.

Do I need to count calories forever?

No. Most people track for a few weeks or months to learn portion sizes and food values, then maintain their results with mindful eating. Tracking is a learning tool, not a life sentence.

Why am I not losing weight at my calculated calories?

Common reasons: underestimating portions, overestimating activity level, or water retention masking fat loss. Track honestly for 2–3 weeks, and if weight hasn't moved, reduce by another 100–200 kcal.

Are all calories the same?

For weight loss, yes — a calorie is a calorie. But for health and hunger, no. Protein keeps you fullest, fiber-rich carbs digest slowly, and highly processed foods are easy to overeat. Quality matters for how you feel.

Is my data saved when I use this calculator?

No. All calculations run entirely in your browser. Your details and results are never stored or sent anywhere.
