The Science of Weight Loss

Everything you need to know about calorie deficits and realistic timelines.

🧮 How Does This Calculator Work?

This calculator combines two proven methods. First, it calculates your TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation — the most accurate formula for estimating how many calories your body burns each day. Then it subtracts your chosen deficit to find your daily calorie target.

Your timeline is based on a simple rule of energy balance: 1 kg of body fat stores roughly 7,700 calories (about 3,500 per pound). So a 500 kcal daily deficit adds up to 3,500 kcal per week — about 0.45–0.5 kg of fat loss weekly.

📉 Which Deficit Should You Choose?

Our advice: Slower is almost always better. Research consistently shows that people who lose weight gradually keep it off far more successfully than rapid dieters.

🛡️ How to Lose Fat Without Losing Muscle

  • Eat enough protein: Aim for 1.6–2.2 g per kg of body weight daily. Protein protects muscle in a deficit.
  • Lift weights: Resistance training 2–4 times per week tells your body to keep muscle and burn fat instead.
  • Don't crash diet: Deficits above 1,000 kcal/day dramatically increase muscle loss.
  • Sleep 7–9 hours: Poor sleep increases hunger hormones and muscle breakdown.

📅 Why Your Timeline Is an Estimate

Real weight loss is never a straight line. Your actual progress will vary because:

  • Water weight fluctuates — you can swing 1–2 kg in a day from salt, carbs, and hydration.
  • Your TDEE drops as you lose weight — a lighter body burns fewer calories, so the same deficit shrinks over time.
  • Metabolic adaptation — your body becomes slightly more efficient during long diets.

Best practice: Weigh yourself 2–3 times per week in the morning, use the weekly average, and recalculate your calories every 4–6 weeks. Add 10–20% buffer time to any goal date.

🚧 Breaking Through a Weight Loss Plateau

Nearly everyone hits a plateau. When the scale stops moving for 2–3 weeks:

  • Recalculate your TDEE at your new, lower weight
  • Track your food honestly for a week — portions creep up over time
  • Add daily steps (8,000–10,000) before cutting calories further
  • Consider a 1–2 week "diet break" at maintenance calories, then resume
FAQs

Weight Loss Questions, Answered

Quick answers to common questions.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

Eat below your TDEE (maintenance calories). A deficit of 500 kcal per day leads to roughly 0.5 kg of fat loss per week. Never go below 1,200 kcal (women) or 1,500 kcal (men) without medical supervision.

How long will it take to lose 10 kg?

At a safe rate of 0.5 kg per week (500 kcal daily deficit), losing 10 kg takes about 20 weeks or 5 months. Faster loss is possible but increases muscle loss and the chance of regaining weight.

What is a calorie deficit?

A calorie deficit means eating fewer calories than your body burns. When this happens, your body uses stored fat for energy, which leads to weight loss. One kg of body fat equals roughly 7,700 calories.

Why did my weight loss stop after a few weeks?

As you lose weight, your TDEE drops — a lighter body burns fewer calories. Your original deficit shrinks, so progress slows. Recalculate your calories every 4–6 weeks or after every 5 kg lost.

Is it safe to lose 1 kg per week?

For people with significant weight to lose, 1 kg per week can be safe short-term with high protein and strength training. For most people, 0.5 kg per week is more sustainable and better preserves muscle.

Is my data saved when I use this calculator?

No. All calculations run entirely in your browser. Your weight, goals, and results are never stored or sent anywhere.
