Understanding Body Fat

Why this number matters more than the scale.

🧮 How Does the US Navy Method Work?

This calculator uses the US Navy circumference method — a formula developed for the military to estimate body fat using simple tape measurements. It compares your waist and neck (plus hips for women) to your height, because the relationship between these circumferences reliably predicts body density.

When measurements are taken correctly, the Navy method is typically accurate within 3–4% of lab-based methods like DEXA scans — making it one of the best free, at-home options available. No expensive equipment, no appointments, just a tape measure and 60 seconds.

Why are women's numbers higher? Women naturally carry more essential fat — it's required for hormonal function and reproductive health. A woman at 22% body fat is comparably lean to a man at 14%.

📏 How to Measure Correctly

  • Neck: Wrap the tape just below the Adam's apple, keeping it level. Don't flare your neck muscles.
  • Waist: Measure at the level of your navel while standing relaxed. Breathe out normally — never suck in your stomach.
  • Hips (women): Measure around the widest point of your hips and buttocks.
  • Tape tension: Snug against the skin, but not digging in.
  • Timing: Measure in the morning before eating, and take each measurement twice to confirm.

⚖️ Body Fat vs BMI: Which Should You Trust?

BMI only compares your weight to your height — it can't tell muscle from fat. Body fat percentage tells you what your weight is actually made of. A muscular athlete can have an "overweight" BMI with 12% body fat, while a sedentary person can have a "healthy" BMI with 30% body fat (sometimes called being "skinny fat").

Best approach: Use both. BMI is a quick screen; body fat percentage tells the real story. If the two disagree, body fat is usually the more meaningful number — especially if you exercise.

🔥 How to Lower Body Fat Safely

  • Moderate calorie deficit: Eat 400–500 kcal below maintenance for roughly 0.5 kg of fat loss per week.
  • High protein: 1.6–2.2 g per kg of body weight daily protects muscle while you lose fat.
  • Strength training: 2–4 sessions per week ensures the weight you lose is fat, not muscle.
  • Daily steps: 8,000–10,000 steps add significant calorie burn without exhausting you.
  • Sleep 7–9 hours: Poor sleep raises hunger hormones and increases fat storage.
  • Be patient: Dropping 1% body fat typically takes 3–6 weeks of consistent effort. Recheck every 2–4 weeks under the same conditions.
FAQs

Body Fat Questions, Answered

Quick answers to common body fat questions.

What is a healthy body fat percentage?

For men, a healthy range is roughly 14–24%; for women, roughly 21–31%. Athletes run lower (6–13% men, 14–20% women), while essential fat minimums are about 2–5% for men and 10–13% for women.

How accurate is the US Navy body fat method?

The Navy tape method is typically accurate within 3–4% of lab methods like DEXA scans when measurements are taken correctly. It's one of the best free, at-home options available.

How do I measure my waist, neck, and hips correctly?

Measure your neck just below the Adam's apple, your waist at the navel (standing relaxed, not sucking in), and hips at the widest point. Keep the tape snug but not tight, and measure in the morning for consistency.

Is body fat percentage better than BMI?

For most active people, yes. Body fat percentage tells you what your weight is made of — fat versus muscle — while BMI only compares weight to height. Two people with the same BMI can have very different body compositions.

How often should I measure my body fat?

Every 2–4 weeks is ideal. Body fat changes slowly, and daily measurements will only show noise from water and measurement error. Always measure under the same conditions.

Can I have a healthy BMI but high body fat?

Yes — it's called being "skinny fat" (normal weight obesity). If you have low muscle mass, your BMI can look healthy while your body fat is too high. This is why body fat percentage is worth tracking alongside BMI.
